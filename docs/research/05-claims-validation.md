# 05 — Claims Validation

Compiled: 17 Oct 2026. All sources fetched and read via live web extraction (URLs in Raw links log). Verdicts: PASS = defensible with cited evidence; REWORD = true in a narrower form; RISKY = unverifiable or contradicted externally; UNSUPPORTED = no citable basis found.

## Verdict per claim

### 1. "India's first express layer for heavy shipments"
**Verdict: RISKY (likely contradicted — reword or scope tightly)**
- Amazon India has run a rail express network for packages since 2019: "scaled its operations from one train in 2019 to 120+ trains in 2024, operating 130 intercity routes across 91 unique destination cities through railways... a 15-time increase" — Amazon/PTI press release, 29 Aug 2024 (https://money.rediff.com/news/market/amazon-indian-railways-partner-for-faster-deliveries/14964820240829). Amazon was "the first firm in the Indian e-commerce sector to partner with the national transporter to build an express transportation solution via rail".
- Rail-first express players already operate: RaillExpress ("Trusted rail logistics across India", 11 trains leased, 60+ stations, 2 lakh shipments/yr, estd 2026 — https://www.raillexpress.in/); RailParcel (rail parcel platform, pilot in 17 locations incl. Hyderabad/Bengaluru/Chennai — https://www.railparcel.co.in/); Bholenath Logistics (rail freight + Next-Day/2nd-Day Express via air & train — https://www.bholenathlogistics.in/).
- Indian Railways itself: Joint Parcel Product (JPP) with India Post (35–100 kg B2C/B2B, pilot 2022), JPP–Rapid Cargo Service policy using BCN/BCNA wagons for parcel traffic (Railway Board policy, 16.12.2024 — https://indianrailways.gov.in/railwayboard/view_section_new.jsp?lang=0&id=0,1,304,366,555,862,2998), CONCOR door-to-door JPP-RCS on Delhi–Kolkata and Mumbai–Kolkata sectors (PIB — https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleaseDetail.aspx?PRID=2238920).
- Gati positions "Multi-Modal (Air, Rail, Road)" express distribution (https://gati.com/new-landing/express-distribution/).
- **Suggested safer wording:** "A rail-first express network built for heavy B2B shipments (200kg+), running parcel-speed linehaul on Indian Railways" — drop "first", or scope as "first rail-first express network purpose-built for 200kg+ B2B payloads" and accept residual risk (RaillExpress markets to heavy machinery/cargo too). "First" is not falsifiable in a pre-seed market without a category definition.

### 2. "80% less carbon" vs road freight
**Verdict: PASS (conservative vs all citable truck classes; can even say ~85–90%)**
- Government of India, Ministry of Railways, Lok Sabha Unstarred Question 5344 (answered 25.03.2026): "Road 101 gm / Rail 11.5 gm (about 89% less) CO2 per tonne per Km — Ref: NITI Aayog Report 'Fast Tracking Freight in India', June 2021" (https://sansad.in/getFile/loksabhaquestions/annex/187/AU5344_v2gz2c.pdf). Same answer: 1 MMT shifted road→rail saves ~5.37 crore kg CO2; rail ₹1.96/tkm vs road ₹3.78/tkm (NCAER Logistics Cost study, Sept 2025, for DPIIT).
- Smart Freight Centre India Default GHG Emission Values v1.0 (May 2025, co-developed with TCI-IIMB Supply Chain Sustainability Lab, ISO 14083/GLEC-aligned): rail WTW = 0.0106 kgCO2e/tkm (10.6 g); road HDT classes WTW = 55.1–82.3 gCO2e/tkm (tractor-trailer 55.1; 20–30t GVW 82.3; 30–50t 66.3); LCVs 380–490 g. → rail is ~81% lower vs tractor-trailer, ~87% vs 20–30t HDT, ~97% vs LCV (https://smart-freight-centre-media.s3.amazonaws.com/documents/India_Default_GHG_Emission_Values_SFC_INDIA_v1_2.pdf).
- ADB TA report (Dec 2023): "Rail transport emits 17-gram CO2..." and "Compared to roads, railways consume 75%–90% less energy for freight traffic" (https://www.adb.org/sites/default/files/project-documents/57027/57027-001-tar-en.pdf).
- **Suggested wording:** keep "up to 80% less carbon" (safe floor) or strengthen to "~85–90% less CO2 per tonne-km vs road" with footnote citing NITI Aayog 2021 + SFC 2025. Add "per tonne-km, WTW basis" as fineprint.

### 3. "99.2% SLA adherence across all live lanes"
**Verdict: RISKY (not externally verifiable; publishable only with definition + audit + fineprint)**
- Industry benchmarks: top Indian logistics players benchmark OTIF ≥95% on-time (Edgistify — https://www.edgistify.com/resources/blogs/otif-b2b-logistics); Tier-2/3 OTD reality 80–88%.
- Blue Dart published "Net Service Levels of 99.96%" (2009, time-definite air express with Money Back Guarantee — https://api.bluedart.com/press145).
- Delhivery (India's largest 3PL) publishes "91%+ service reliability across our entire network" (AR FY23 — https://www.delhivery.com/uploads/2023/09/Delhivery_Limited_AR-2022-23.pdf) and "average fulfilment time of ~3.2 days delivered across India" (AR FY25 — https://www.delhivery.com/uploads/2025/08/Annual_Report_FY25.pdf).
- Porter explicitly disclaims time-bound completion (see fineprint). Most Indian freight players publish no OTD % at all.
- **Suggested wording:** keep 99.2% only if internally audited with a published definition ("% of shipments arriving within committed window, last 90 days, all live lanes"), attach a money-back/credit remedy, and footnote methodology. Otherwise "industry-leading SLA adherence backed by per-lane commitments" is safer.

### 4. "1.4T CO2 saved per lane vs road"
**Verdict: UNSUPPORTED as written (unit/period ambiguous; no basis shown)**
- Worked math (SFC intensities, 200kg payload, Hyderabad→Bangalore ~570 km): road HDT 20–30t @ 82.3 g/tkm × 0.2 t × 570 km ≈ 9.4 kg CO2e; rail @ 10.6 g/tkm ≈ 1.2 kg → **~8.2 kg CO2e saved per 200kg shipment**. 1.4 tonnes saved per lane requires ~170 such shipments (or ~35 at 1-tonne payloads).
- **Suggested wording:** replace with per-shipment stat ("~8 kg CO2e saved per 200 kg shipment on HYD–BLR") or state the period ("1.4 tonnes CO2e saved per lane in [month], basis: [N] shipments, SFC India intensities, WTW").

### 5. Lane TATs (e.g. Hyderabad→Bangalore "24hrs vs 3 days")
**Verdict: REWORD (3-day road baseline overstated for FTL on HYD–BLR; defensible for LTL/consolidated and for longer lanes)**
- HYD→BLR is 563–570 km via NH-44. FTL truck: 7–9 hrs non-stop, 10–13 hrs door-to-door (TruckGuru — https://truckguru.co.in/hyderabad-to-bangalore-transport-service). Packers/movers: 8–10 hrs transit, "typically delivered overnight or in 24–36 hours"; shared/consolidated trucks add "1–3 days" (PackersBazaar — https://packersbazaar.in/route/hyderabad-to-bangalore). → FTL road is already ~24h door-to-door; "vs 3 days" only holds vs LTL/PTL consolidation. Delhivery confirms PTL transit is slower than parcel on the same lane due to consolidation (https://www.delhivery.com/blog/when-to-switch-express-to-ptl).
- DEL→Mumbai (1,400–1,450 km): 22–26 hrs pure driving (PackersBazaar) but standard FTL ops norm is 300–350 km/day (URL Safe Express — https://urlsafe.in/routes/delhi-mumbai/) → ~4–5 days typical. Here "rail 2 days vs road 4–5 days" is credible.
- **Suggested wording:** per-lane table with explicit baseline: "HYD–BLR: 24h vs 1–3 days LTL/PTL" and "DEL–BOM: 2 days vs 4–5 days FTL norm". Do not claim 3-day FTL baseline on HYD–BLR.

### 6. "Cut transit to 2 days, distributors rotate cash 3x faster"
**Verdict: REWORD (only holds on 3x-transit lanes and only for in-transit/pipeline inventory)**
- Inventory turnover = COGS ÷ average inventory; days-in-inventory = 365 ÷ turnover (CFI — https://corporatefinanceinstitute.com/resources/accounting/inventory-turnover/). Cash conversion cycle = DIO + DSO − DPO (Investopedia — https://www.investopedia.com/terms/w/workingcapitalmanagement.asp). A transit cut improves turnover only in proportion to transit's share of the replenishment cycle: if a distributor reorders weekly, cutting transit 3d→1d lifts turnover ~1.25x, not 3x. 3x rotation requires the *entire* cycle to shrink 3x — true only where road transit ≥3x rail transit (HYD–BLR LTL 3d vs 24h = 3x; DEL–BOM 4–6d vs 2d ≈ 2–3x) and only for the in-transit capital, not total stock.
- **Suggested wording:** "up to 3x faster rotation of in-transit working capital on 3x-transit lanes (e.g. HYD–BLR)" with a footnote on pipeline-inventory math.

### 7. "Parcel-speed for 200kg payloads" (consumer expectation)
**Verdict: PASS (as narrative; consumer-expectation data is real, though it is B2C parcel/grocery data extended to heavy B2B)**
- Datum Intelligence (Dec 2024): "nearly 7 out of 10 online grocery buyers (69%) now favour 10-minute delivery over next-day scheduling" (https://www.indiaretailing.com/2024/12/05/fast-delivery-drive-quick-commerce/).
- PwC India survey (Dec 2024–Jan 2025, 1,000+ retailers, 800 consumers): 42% of metro consumers have ditched traditional shopping for quick commerce (via Fortune India — https://www.fortuneindia.com/business-news/quick-commerce-takes-over-cities-42-in-metros-have-ditched-traditional-shopping-says-pwc/); report PDF: https://www.pwc.in/assets/pdfs/retail-reinvention-paradigm-v1.pdf.
- Bain & Flipkart "How India Shops Online 2025": quick commerce (<30-min delivery) is the #1 disruption reshaping India's e-retail (https://storage.googleapis.com/flipkart-stories-media/uploads/bain_report_how_india_shops_online_2025_e8b72ad2e8.pdf).
- **Suggested wording:** keep, but phrase as expectation-setting narrative ("the same expectation is now hitting heavy B2B"), not as a measured statistic for 200kg payloads.

### 8. "Quick-commerce killed consumer patience"
**Verdict: PASS (narrative, well supported — soften to 'reset expectations')**
- Same sources as #7. Recommended phrasing: "Quick commerce reset delivery expectations across Indian commerce" — "killed patience" is rhetorical and fine on a landing page but avoid implying a measured causal claim.

| DB Group/Schenker | "We aim to be climate-neutral by 2040" (DB Group ibir); Schenker brand now DSV post-2025 acquisition | 2040 net-zero value chain | corporate target, not per-shipment |

| Flock Freight | Shared Truckload "slashing carbon emissions by **up to 40%** compared to traditional shipping methods" | up to 40% | SFC-accredited, quarterly GLEC-compliant emissions reports, B Corp |

**Language patterns that work:** time-definite product names + money-back (BlueDart); "up to X%" for carbon (Flock, DHL, sennder); "on average"/"across our entire network" (Delhivery); third-party validation (SBTi for Maersk, GLEC/SFC for Flock) to make carbon claims credible; named-customer case studies for lane-level claims (sennder).

## Fineprint precedents (quoted)

1. **Porter T&C, clause 19** (https://porter.in/terms-of-service): "No commitment is made for time bound completion of service. Any time of arrival provided at booking / completion of Service is an estimate only and, there may be occasional del[ays]…"
2. **Delhivery Direct PTL T&C** (https://www.delhivery.com/tnc-direct-ptl): "We aim to deliver your Shipment within the Estimated Time of Delivery." Plus: "Delhivery may route shipments via any method/partner it deems fit"; claims must be recorded on POD at handover, "claims raised after 72 hours of Shipment closure will not be considered"; 4-day free storage then ₹1/kg/day demurrage.
3. **BlueDart MBG conditions** (https://api.bluedart.com/press145): "In case of a delivery failure within the stipulated time, the customer will be entitled to a full refund" — applies only to Domestic Priority 1030 / Dart Apex 1200, select origin/destination pin codes.
4. **Delhivery AR hedges** (https://www.delhivery.com/uploads/2023/09/Delhivery_Limited_AR-2022-23.pdf): "overall network speed improved to 3.6 days, on average with 91%+ service reliability across our entire network."
5. **DHL GoGreen Plus** (https://www.dhl.com/discover/en-jp/ship-with-dhl/gogreen-plus): "SAF usage enables **up to 80%** reduced life cycle emissions compared to conventional jet fuel" — "up to", inset (not offset) framing, certificates for Scope 3 reporting.
6. **Flock Freight** (https://www.flockfreight.com/company/sustainability/overview): "slashing carbon emissions by up to 40% compared to traditional shipping methods" + "quarterly GLEC-compliant emissions reports" (SFC accreditation).
7. **TERI/GIZ GHG Calculator methodology** (https://www.teriin.org/sites/default/files/2022-10/Freight_GHG_Calculator_Methodology_Report.pdf): "GIZ does not, however, guarantee the accuracy or completeness of information in this document" — standard research-methodology disclaimer pattern.

## Market data pack (stat → value → source → date)

- India e-retail GMV: **$60B (2024) → $170–190B projected 2030** — Bain & Flipkart, How India Shops Online 2025.
- Quick commerce market: **$6.1B CY2024 → ₹1.07 lakh Cr ($12.9B) CY2025E, +111% YoY** — Datum Intelligence Quick Commerce India 2024 survey (Oct 2024).
- Quick commerce definition: **<30-minute delivery**, named #1 disruption by Bain 2025.
- Consumer speed expectation: **69% of online grocery buyers prefer 10-min over next-day** — Datum Intelligence via IndiaRetailing, 5 Dec 2024.
- **42% of metro consumers ditched traditional shopping** for quick commerce — PwC India survey (Dec 2024–Jan 2025, 1,000+ retailers, 800 consumers), via Fortune India.
- Small home appliances (heavy-adjacent durable): **₹138 Bn FY25 → ~₹200 Bn FY30, ~8% CAGR** — RedSeer, "Disruption in the Indian Small Home Appliances Category", 15 Oct 2026.
- India household appliances market: **~$23.7–25B (2025)** — Mordor Intelligence / TechSci Research (secondary aggregators).
- Freight modal split: **road 66%, rail 22%, waterways 8%** — NITI Aayog, Scenarios Towards Viksit Bharat & Net-Zero: Sectoral Insights — Transport (Feb 2026). IR freight loading: **1,617 MT FY2024-25** (MoR Lok Sabha answer, 25 Mar 2026).
- **Rail 11.5 g vs road 101 g CO2/tonne-km (= ~89% less)** — NITI Aayog "Fast Tracking Freight in India" (Jun 2021), quoted in MoR Lok Sabha USQ 5344 (25.03.2026).
- SFC India intensities (May 2025): rail WTW **10.6 gCO2e/tkm**; road HDT **55–82 gCO2e/tkm**; LCV 380–490.
- 1 MMT road→rail saves **~5.37 crore kg CO2**; logistics cost **₹1.96/tkm rail vs ₹3.78/tkm road** — NCAER for DPIIT (Sep 2025), via Lok Sabha USQ 5344.
- Gati Shakti Cargo Terminals: **142 commissioned + 310 IPA, 224 MTPA capacity, ₹10,000 Cr private investment** — PIB, 23 Jul 2026 (https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2288380).
- Amazon rail scale: **1 train (2019) → 120+ trains (2024), 130 routes, 91 cities, 15x parcel volume** — Amazon PR via PTI/Rediff, 29 Aug 2024.
- IR parcel infrastructure: JPP with India Post (35–100 kg, pilot 2022); **JPP-RCS policy 16.12.2024** (BCN/BCNA wagons for parcels); CONCOR door-to-door JPP-RCS on Delhi–Kolkata/Mumbai–Kolkata; 'Rail Parcel' app pilot — PIB PRID 2238920.
- SLA norms: **OTIF ≥95% = top-player benchmark; Tier-2/3 OTD reality 80–88%** — Edgistify. **BlueDart NSL 99.96%** (2009). **Delhivery 91%+ reliability, 3.6 days avg (FY23); ~3.2 days (FY25)**.
- Lane transit (road baseline): **HYD–BLR 563–570 km: FTL 7–9h non-stop / 10–13h door-to-door** (TruckGuru); movers 8–10h, overnight–36h (PackersBazaar); LTL/consolidated +1–3 days. **DEL–BOM 1,400–1,450 km: 22–26h driving** (PackersBazaar) but **FTL ops norm 300–350 km/day → ~4–5 days** (URL Safe Express).
- Rail energy/emissions context: rail carries ~1/3 of India's freight tonnage but ~10% of transport CO2 (IEA, 2016); ADB (2023): rail uses 75–90% less energy than road for freight.
